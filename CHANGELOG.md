# Changelog

## 1.3.0

ControllerCam
- Fishing has a camera of its own from the start: when you cast your line the view comes in close over your shoulder (the Shooter profile), looking out at the float, and holds between casts while the pole stays in your hands. Only carrying a pole changes nothing, and putting it away ends it. `/ccam set fishing <profile>` picks another, `/ccam set fishing none` none. An install from before gets it once, unless a camera was already chosen for fishing.

ControllerCam: HUD
- Fishing front and centre: while you fish, the cast bar moves to the middle of the screen and is drawn half as large again, so the line, the float and what bites here are the main event (switch: **Fishing front and centre**, `/cchud fishbig`).
- Any button can be the fishing button, a trigger too: `/cchud fishkey rt` (or `x`, `lt`, `rb`...). A button that is not your interact button casts whenever your line is in, and hooks the fish by the game's own Interact while the float is out.
- The cast bar is drawn larger (135%), so the fishing float and what plays on the bar are easier to make out. "Cast bar size" sets it (`/cchud castsize`), and `/cchud sizefor monitor` brings it back to plain size along with the text.
- While you fish, the bright edge that rides the front of other casts is hidden: it stood on the float and the rings round it. The word "Fishing" is gone from the bar as well (the time stays), and a loose line now leads up from the top of the float to where a rod's tip would be, slack and swaying, shorter as the float comes in.
- The float on the "casts" hint, before your line is out, is more than twice the size.
- A fish journal (`/cchud journal`, or its key binding; switch: **Fish journal**). Every catch is written down: how many of each kind, where and when you first landed it, and a weight. The game has no weights, so they are made up for fun: each kind has its own usual size, each catch falls around it, a big one is rare, and a fish that names its weight ("17 Pound Catfish") weighs that. A new kind and a new heaviest are said in chat. The window has two pages, Fish and Waters, with a few lines of lore about many of the old world's fish and about each of its zones, shown once you have landed something there, in all ten languages. D-pad chooses, a shoulder button turns the page, B closes.
- A fishing interface under the line while a pole is in your hands (switch: **Fishing interface**, `/cchud fishpanel`): the fish of the water you stand at, each a "?" until you have landed it there (with Improved Forever's fishing data loaded the whole list of the place is known; without it the list is what you have landed there, with room left); your Fishing skill as a small bar with what bait and pole add to it, "150 (+50) / 225"; how many you caught and how many got away since you logged in; the bait on your pole and how long it lasts; and to the right your latest catches with their weights. It is fainter between casts and gone in a fight.
- The ones that got away are counted: when the game says so, and when your line runs its whole time with nothing landed. Casting again early does not count.
- Bait on a double press (switch: **Bait on a double press**, `/cchud fishbait`): press your fishing button twice quickly and the second press puts the best bait in your bags on the pole, when the pole has none. For the half second after your cast begins the button uses the bait; after that it is the hook as before. No fish bites that early.
- A game while the float is out, off unless you switch it on (**A game while the float is out**, `/cchud fishgame`): a small stretch of water beside the fishing interface, your lure in it and the shadow of a fish drifting up and down. Hold the left trigger and the lure sinks, let go and it rises (the right trigger when the left one is your fishing button). Keep the lure beside the shadow and a meter fills; whatever you land next is up to 60% heavier in the journal, and the meter starts again. It carries over from cast to cast, a fish that gets away costs half of it, and catches in a row make the shadow quicker. It binds nothing and takes no button: the trigger is only looked at, and your cast and your hook are untouched.
- The quest list can be thinned out and put in order, each a switch of its own on the Quests, chat and map page. **No group quests while alone** leaves elite, dungeon and raid quests out while you are on your own (one that is ready to hand in stays). **Only the nearest** lists no more than so many quests, the nearest ones (`/cchud questnear 3`). **Nearest first** keeps the list in order of distance, with the game's own sort where it has one. **Recommended order** puts ready-to-hand-in first, then what you can do alone nearest first, then quests above your level, group quests while you are alone, and grey quests last. `/cchud questplan` says the recommended order for your whole quest log in chat. As with "current zone only", it is the game's own list driven through the game's own tracking, and your own tracking comes back when the switches are off.
- Button icons: the game shows your controller's buttons as numbers, letters or shapes, and starts every controller on numbers. The HUD now sets the game's own style for the controller in use: found by itself (PlayStation: shapes; Xbox and Steam: letters; Switch: letters the other way round), or the kind you choose with the six buttons on the main options page or `/cchud padicons auto | xbox | playstation | switch | steam | generic | game`. A controller that does not say what it is keeps the numbers, a style somebody set by hand is not changed by "auto", and `game` (or switching the HUD off) puts back what the game had.
- Shields show as a ward (switch: **Shields show as a ward**, `/cchud wards`). A shield is cast in an instant, so there is nothing of it on a cast bar: instead a ward flares round the health bar of whoever has it (your target, your focus, you), glows while the shield holds and fades when it breaks. Gold for the holy shields (Power Word: Shield, Divine Shield, Blessing of Protection), pale blue for Ice Barrier, violet for Mana Shield.
- Party nameplates in dungeons and raids. In there the game draws friendly players' nameplates itself and no addon may touch them, so your party wore the game's bars in the middle of our look. The game has options of its own for this, and the HUD now sets them while you are inside: friendly players show as their name alone, in their class colour, with no bar, which is how our own friendly nameplates look, and for a fight their bars come back, as ours do (the game's own bars: that part cannot be ours; it follows **Bars for friends in a fight**). The switch is **Party nameplates in dungeons: names only**, on to begin with; **Party nameplates in dungeons: hidden** switches them off altogether instead. Each setting is put back the way you had it when you leave, and one you change yourself while inside is left as you set it. In the open world and in battlegrounds friendly nameplates wear our look as before.
- Fixed: in a fight the game refused to move a frame of ours that its own aura rows hang on, and the HUD kept asking every frame of a hit shake. Such a frame is now left where it is until the fight is over; the game is asked once.
- L3 is never taken from you: it stays your auto-run. At the bank, a vendor, in a trade or at the mailbox, **R3** now moves the item you point at, and sorts when you point at nothing (it was L3 that moved). The Market at the auction house no longer takes L3 either: point at its tab, or bind "Market" to a key of your own.
- The Market and the fish journal take their controller buttons by key bindings of their own while they are open (the d-pad, B and the few others they use), cleared when they close or a fight starts. Before, they caught every press and passed the rest on to the game, and the game could then refuse its own actions (interact, cancel, an action button) for the rest of the session.
- The vertical bars start a little further right, closer to your character (bars you placed yourself stay).
- Buffs rest with the rest of the screen out of combat, like the minimap and the gamepad bars (**Rests: buffs**, under Advanced).
- Chat: no gap between the class icon and the level in the brackets.
- Fixed: where the game keeps health hidden from addons (in a dungeon), the health percent on the target frame is now asked of the game on a 0-100 scale instead of being worked out, which the game refused.
- Tooltips are mostly see-through now, so one at your cursor does not cover what you are looking at: **Tooltip background** on the Look page (`/cchud tooltipglass 0-100`) sets how solid their background and border are, from the full dark glass (100) to text alone (0). It starts at 40. Without the HUD's tooltip look it fades the game's own tooltip background instead.
- The nameplate report lists the game's own nameplate settings and how many plates the game keeps to itself.
- With debug reports on, the game's own record of refused actions is kept as well (`Logs/taint.log`, its "taintLog" setting, put back when the reports are switched off), and a refused action is saved with the path that led to it.
- Options: fishing has a page of its own, and "The game's own options" is now **Native Options**.
- The debug reports gained what these need: the fishing book and interface, the quest list, the button icons, and for anything the game refuses, which lines asked for it.

## 1.2.0

ControllerCam
- Shapeshift forms (druid forms, ghost wolf) no longer push the camera further to the side: the game scales the shoulder offset with your model, so in a form it is scaled back. `/ccam formoffset <percent>` tunes it for the form you are in.
- Two more situations: **Indoors** and **Fishing** (a fishing pole in hand, or the fishing cast). Both are empty until you pick a profile for them, for example `/ccam set indoors shooter` for a closer over-the-shoulder view inside buildings.

ControllerCam: HUD (new, optional second addon in the same download)
- It asks before it does anything: the first time it loads beside ControllerCam it rests and asks **Camera + HUD** or **Camera only**. Until you answer, and after "Camera only", the game's interface is exactly as it was. `/cchud on`, `/cchud off` or the Enabled switch in its options answer the question just the same, and `/cchud welcome` asks again. Installed on its own, or with settings from before, it does not ask. A second question follows a yes, and comes once to anyone using the HUD on one of the game's built-in layouts: shall the screen be set up for the controller? It adds a layout named CCHUD to the game's Edit Mode, with the cooldown rows under the target and clear of the gamepad buttons, for you to choose there (the game does not let addons choose a layout, so you are told where to pick it); your own layouts are kept, and someone who arranged a layout of their own is not asked. `/cchud setup` offers it again. Picking a preset adds it too if you are still on a built-in layout, and never overwrites one you have arranged since. `/cchud reset all` puts everything back to how it started: every setting, the frames, and that layout as it was made; your vendor marks and price history are kept.
- Part of the ControllerCam family: its options sit under ControllerCam's (Options > AddOns > ControllerCam > HUD, with its pages under it), the addon list groups it with ControllerCam on clients that group addons, and its main page starts with a Modules list to switch each part on or off. Without the camera addon it keeps its own place in the options.
- A controller interface that takes over while the Gamepad UI is in use and gives everything back in the mouse and keyboard UI, or when it is turned off or removed.
- Slim player, pet, target, target-of-target and focus frames with class colours, a damage trail, hit flashes, a low-health pulse and a combat glow. They can be dragged in the game's Edit Mode.
- Party and raid frames, nameplates and the cast bar get the same look, drawn over the game's own frames, which keep doing the targeting and keep their options and positions.
- If our party and raid look cannot find the game's member frames, the game's own frames stay in sight instead of disappearing.
- The game's own player, target and focus frames are made invisible but stay where the game keeps them, so the controller's unit menu (leave party, inspect, trade...) keeps working.
- Party frames no longer break when the game keeps a party member's range or status secret ("attempt to perform boolean test on a secret boolean"): range fading is then done by the game itself, unseen by us.
- NPCs whose job is in their name ("Auctioneer Tricket", "Innkeeper Norman") get their icon too.
- Party frames of our own when the game keeps its party frames hidden in the Gamepad UI: secure (click to target, the unit menu), stacked on the left under the focus frame.
- Hit effects also fire from a health drop on your target and on nameplates, for clients that send addons no hit events.
- Nameplates show only your own debuffs (a client that ignores the "yours" filter no longer shows everyone's); the quest marker is the icon alone; a name can no longer go missing on your target's plate.
- Enemy casts on nameplates start with a flash and a swell, then pulse ever faster until they land (calm for casts that cannot be interrupted).
- Threat as a shield icon and a colour, on nameplates and the target frame alike: red and strong when it is on you (shield with "!"), red and yellow in turn when you are about to pull it (arrow up), yellow and blue in turn when you are losing it (arrow down), blue when it is not on you. The colour glows behind the plate's name and around the target bar. Your share as a number beside the icon is optional (`/cchud threatpercent`). The level keeps its difficulty colour. Without threat numbers, the unit's own target tells it.
- Rank in the classic style: gold wings around an elite's name, silver for a rare, silver wings on a gold glow for a rare elite, and a skull with gold wings for a boss.
- Compact nameplates and a compact target frame (about 60% of the width).
- Nameplates show a power bar only for units that have a resource (no empty track under monsters that never build rage or energy).
- The screen rests out of combat, all or nothing: the gamepad bars, the minimap, the quest list, the damage meter and the experience bar fade together (the game has no option for it) and come back together for a fight, a pulled trigger (and a few seconds after: a pull of the trigger is how you look at them), an enemy target, the controller's cursor or the mouse over one of them. One switch (**Fade out of combat**) and one level (**The screen at rest**); each part can be left out under Advanced. What you had chosen for the gamepad bars' own fade carries over. `/cchud compact` switches the bars between compact and full (the game's own Edit Mode setting), also from the options.
- The stance and pet command buttons move up to where the green and red targeting icons sat, and those icons are hidden: the wheel a button opens (stances, forms, pet commands) is no longer cut off by the bottom of the screen. The buttons stay the game's own; they are moved out of combat only, and put back in Edit Mode or with **Stance and pet buttons above the bars** off. `/cchud padinfo` lists where the bars' parts sit, for troubleshooting.
- Debug reports, off unless you switch them on (`/cchud debug on`): the troubleshooting reports then gather themselves when you log in and again at the moment you reload or log out, with short notes of what the HUD did in between, all in the saved settings. Nothing leaves your computer.
- Your bars have an in-combat level (70% by default) and come back in full at a call to action: health below 50% or mana below 30%. The focus frame steps back out of combat too.
- Minimal keeps your focus and pet frames and hides them out of combat; Action fades them.
- The game's pop-up buff tooltip is switched off once (it covered the target frame); switch it back on in the options.
- Effects are read the way this client allows: the unit's whole list, with the game itself picking which are yours and which are crowd control (reading them one by one is refused when their details are secret, which hid your debuffs). Clients without those calls still read them one by one, and an effect from a spell you cast lately counts as yours there. `/cchud aurareport` lists what the game says, for troubleshooting.
- The quest marker sits on the health bar's line.
- No more hooks on the game's own frames (gamepad bars, player, target and focus frames, nameplates): our alpha is kept by a timer of our own instead, so the game's code is never tainted ("Interface action failed because of an AddOn").
- Nameplate names stay white and readable at their usual size: threat shows in a strong coloured glow behind the name, which flares when you take aggro and fades when you lose it; with aggro the name is bolder.
- Crowd control on a nameplate sits at the bar's right end, after the threat shield and the quest marker (each moves up when the one before it is not showing), instead of on the left over the level and name.
- Effect countdowns turn red under five seconds, on nameplates and the target frame (the game's own icons and ours alike).
- Effect times no longer overlap: under the target the time sits below each icon with room between them, the game's countdown numbers are drawn smaller, and nameplate icons are spaced a little wider.
- Effects are drawn by the game itself where this client offers it (its aura display element): your debuffs (judgements, Consecration...), the enemy's buffs and crowd control on nameplates and the target frame, with their time and stacks, even when their details are secret to addons. Clients without it keep our own rows.
- "Name on your target's plate" now means the same for every target (before, an enemy attacking you kept its name, so names came and went in a fight). It is switched back on once by this update. A plate whose rank the game keeps secret in a fight still shows its name.
- Stylized numbers for what you take: damage pops in the game's damage font, coloured by its school, and falls away beside your character; heals rise in green; a burst spreads out so each number stays readable. Crits, dodges, parries and misses are the big moments: they slam in huge, stick where they land, then rise and fade, like your own crits. They stand in for the game's scrolling combat text for you and follow its option (off there: off here).
- The game's own frames in the HUD's look while the Gamepad UI is in use (Options > Look, each part on its own switch): the gamepad buttons get a slim ring on a dark backing instead of the heavy art, with a soft pool behind each cluster; tooltips become dark glass edged in a player's class colour or an item's quality; buffs are cropped and edged; the quest tracker's gold headers become glass; the minimap gets a slim ring and halo instead of the gold rim; the experience bar is drawn smaller in its own look; chat tabs and the chat box follow. Nothing of the game's is hooked or replaced: the frames keep working, and switching off, going back to the mouse UI or removing the addon puts every one back as it was.
- `/chud` works as a short name for `/cchud`.
- Controller vibration: a thud when you are hit (heavier for a big hit or a crit), a tick in the right trigger for your crits, a double knock when your target's cast is interrupted, a heartbeat while you are low in a fight, a pulse when your target turns on you, alerts for a ready check, a dungeon invitation or a whisper, a swell at level up and a long rumble at death. Each on its own switch, with a strength slider; the game's own vibration setting comes first (off there: nothing). Putting on a seal charges up: a quick rising rumble that snaps in the right trigger. Optional (off by default): spells with a cast time build up with the cast bar and snap when they land. `/cchud rumbletest` plays every pattern once.
- Signature moments: each class's key abilities have a feel of their own on the controller (a Judgement splashes, a Holy Strike snaps, a Windfury Attack knocks three times; Mortal Strike, Eviscerate, Pyroblast, Aimed Shot, Stormstrike and more), stronger on a crit. Rarely, a really special hit is pinned big and golden in the middle of the screen in the game's damage font, with the ability's name: back-to-back crits of your key abilities, a Windfury crit, or a crit far above the fight's usual; at most twice a fight. `/cchud momenttest` shows one.
- A controller with a light bar (DualSense) can show your health in it: green to red, pulsing when you are low in a fight; it goes back to the game's colour when switched off.
- Casts look like what they are, on your cast bar and the enemies': mining throws chips of stone, herbs shed leaves, skinning, opening, crafting, disenchanting, hearthstones and mounts each have their colour and their little effects, and a spell takes the colour of its school (learned from the combat log) and lands in that school's style.
- Cast bar animations, painted in the game's own style (a dark line, a few flat tones, a glint): picks peck at the end of the bar while you mine (chips fly at every strike) and a hammer while you craft, leaves sprout along it for herbs, green lightning runs along it stroke after stroke for nature and pale blue lightning for lightning spells, roots creep along it for the spells that root a foe, a vine winds along it and flowers open one after another for a heal (gold ones for a holy heal), runes hover over it and take shape one by one for arcane, flames burn along what is filled and grow as the cast builds, ice freezes on crystal by crystal and shatters when the cast lands, hooded hollow-eyed shades drift through a dark haze for shadow, rays rise for holy, and a physical cast keeps the plain bar with only a glint of steel. Every school has its show, and each is kept to a few pieces. Your own bar has the full show, nameplates a lighter one. `/cchud casttest` plays them all.
- Spell schools are known from the start for the spells players cast most (by name, so every rank and language), and learned for the rest from the combat log or from the hit or heal that follows your cast, on clients whose combat log addons cannot read. A spell shows its school's effect while it is cast as well as when it lands.
- Healing you can see: the heal on its way to a unit takes the colour of the heal being cast at it (holy gold, nature green; yours or a group member's), and a heal over time grows over the bar of your target, focus or you while it lasts: roots and branches for nature (Rejuvenation, Regrowth, Lifebloom...), golden threads for holy (Renew). They sway gently and wither away when it ends.
- Heals light up the bar they land on in their school's colour (holy, nature...), with its effect rising off it: your target, focus, nameplates and your own bars.
- Your player frame and vertical bars give a small shake when you are hit (switch: Your bars shake when you are hit).
- The threat shield sits on the health bar's line, just past its end (on the target frame and nameplates, where the quest marker steps aside for it), so the name above has the bar's full width; over the compact target bar it has more room than the bar. The target of target moves out past both, so nothing overlaps.
- A nameplate the game reuses for another unit no longer keeps the last unit's "Interrupted".
- Casts the game names by their inner workings show a plain name: "Opening - No Text" reads "Opening", and opening or gathering says what you work on when the game can name it ("Opening: Sweet Nectar Barrel").
- Enemy cast bars show the spell's icon (greyed when it cannot be interrupted); an interrupted cast shatters in red and says so, a cast that lands bursts, and the bar lingers a moment so you see which.
- Effects drawn by the game (debuffs, buffs, crowd control on nameplates and the target) now show their icons; only their timers showed before.
- Picking a camera preset for everywhere in ControllerCam also picks the matching HUD preset (Action, Classic, Shooter, Raid... each has its match; the camera keeps its own distance). Switch: Follow ControllerCam's presets.
- Health on the bars: full-size target and nameplates show the health left and the percent ("12.3k  85%"), compact ones the percent alone; the target's number sits on its bar, clear of the name.
- Small plates for minor units now include grey enemies that give no experience, and show the name and a slim bar only (no level, rank flourish or health text). Compact bars are switched back to full size once.
- `/cchud errors` lists the last errors the HUD met, also after a reload, to report them.
- Quick switches after the fact: the HUD's main page has a Quick switches section (tooltips at the cursor, compact bars everywhere or one by one), and a key binding flips tooltips at the cursor on and off; also `/cchud compactall` and `/cchud targetcompact`; presets never change it.
- Class icons for players, friend or enemy, on the target frame and now on nameplates too, on by default; presets no longer switch them off, and they come back on once for anyone a preset had switched them off for.
- Presets always give the same result, whatever was chosen before: each one starts from the defaults of everything any preset sets. Most keep the full name data on plates (player titles, guilds and what NPCs do, such as flight masters and trainers); only Soulslike, Shooter and ARPG go without guilds and jobs.
- Presets: four more whole-screen layouts after other kinds of games (Adventure, Crossbar, ARPG, Compass); every preset keeps what you need to play (nameplates with names, levels, threat, debuffs, crowd control and loud casts, your vitals, your target, the cast bar, party frames); Minimal shows friendly NPCs only when you point at them, aim at them or target them; and a preset brings the camera to the distance its layout was made for, once (switch that off in General).
- Players' full names everywhere: the target, target of target, focus, party frames and nameplates all read "Faith Ashborne", last name included (the game hands it over as "Faith-Ashborne", sometimes already joined; the target frame used to drop the last name). A name that already carries its last name (a player's title name does) never gets it twice. Switch: Last names. `/cchud names` shows what the game gives for your target's name.
- The numbers for what you take appear at the vertical bars when those are on, falling away outward.
- The experience bar keeps its own look, drawn smaller (size in Look).
- The minimap's ring is drawn large and sharp; the chat box's glass shows only while you type.
- Bags: sort with one action (a key binding under AddOns, or `/cchud sort`), the bank too while it is open; it is the game's own sorting, so its options count. An item's tooltip says how many you have in your bags and bank and how many your other characters hold, with the total. `/cchud gold` lists every character's gold.
- Junk is judged more carefully: profession tools and fishing poles (a mining pick is a white weapon to the game), gear with a use of its own, and white weapons or armor that could be an upgrade for you (an empty slot, or a higher item level than what you wear) are never junk, unless you mark them as junk yourself. Selling when a vendor opens only sells grey items and your own junk marks by itself; anything else is listed and asked about first. `/cchud sold` lists what was sold this session, for the vendor's Buyback tab.
- Item tooltips show what an item sells for at a vendor, each and for a full stack, and the lowest auction buyout you have seen for it, with how long ago (noted from whatever the auction house shows you, old or new auction house alike).
- Tooltips say more in fewer words, in colour: item counts read "Bag x26  Bank x40  (x80)" (the total across your characters in gold), prices are in the coins' colours, and each stat value has a colour of its own (attack power, ranged attack power, crit, dodge, health, mana, spell crit).
- What stats are worth to you: each stat line in an item's tooltip gets its value for your character in brackets, "+10 Agility (+10 AP, +20 RAP, +0.19% crit, +0.38% dodge)": Strength and Agility into attack power and ranged attack power by class, Agility into crit and dodge, Stamina into health, Intellect into mana and spell crit. Crit and dodge per point come from the game for your level where it says them. Works in every language.

Beside the house a pane that only shows follows your choice: the item's price chart for the last 30 days, its usual price, how many are up, what a vendor pays and the reasoning in full.   - On an enemy you see your own debuffs plus the ones the game marks important.
  - A friendly target now shows its debuffs on the right under its bar: the ones you can dispel, edged in their kind's colour, then boss mechanics, then crowd control.

- Nameplates in dungeons: no more errors where the game keeps units' details secret, and no more plates left showing someone who has gone (a reused plate is cleared first, and plates are checked again every second). The aura icons above a plate stay in one short line, clear of its name.

B or Escape closes it.
