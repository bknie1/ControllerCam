# Changelog

## 1.4.11

ControllerCam: HUD
- Big moments are a feed: a block just above the cooldown callouts on the right of your character (setting: **Big moments: where**, `/cchud momentplace`, buttons **Feed on the right** (the default) and **One in the middle**). Each moment is its own entry, the newest nearest the cooldowns; the older ones move up over it and each fades on its own time, up to four at once. One in the middle is how they showed before. The abilities stand one to a line ("+ Judgement", "+ Holy Strike  ×2"), so a long combo no longer runs under the quest tracker, and the block sits a little higher over the cooldowns.
- The bag window's tooltips work as in the game's own bags. With the controller they follow the game's own tooltip setting, and R3 turns it on and off, as the game's bags do ("R3 Hide tooltip" / "R3 Tooltip" on the button line). With the mouse an item's tooltip always shows. R3 no longer sorts while the bag window has the controller; sorting stays in Y.
- Destroy's question is never covered: the item's tooltip stays away while it asks (the question names the item), and comes back once you choose. It shows one instruction at a time, on its button: "A Hold to destroy", then "Let go to destroy" once the bar is full, with **Keep** under it. Nothing overlaps at any text size. An item that begins a quest says so in red ("This item begins a quest."), as the game's own delete warning does, and so does a quest item. An item a quest in your log still needs is still never offered.

## 1.4.10

ControllerCam: HUD
- Items are kept unless they're clearly junk (setting: **Item advice: when unsure**, `/cchud adviceunsure`, buttons **Keep** (the default) and **Sell**). Only grey items and what you mark as junk (and your own junk rules) are advised for sale, faded as junk in the bag window, or put in the vendor's Smart sale. Everything else says Keep and why; with no other reason it says "kept: nothing says it's junk". Its prices still show, and where the auction house pays well the tooltip says so: "could fetch 7s at the auction house". The Market still lists what you could sell there, with its worth and chance, so the choice is yours. **Sell** is the advice as it was in 1.4.9.
- Fixed: gear for your other spec was called junk. A Holy paladin's Protection sword and shield in the bags were faded as junk and could go in the sell list. Now the best you own for any of your specs (both talent groups always, worn or in your bags) is kept and says so: "Keep: your Protection gear". Anything in one of the game's equipment sets is always kept: "Keep: in your equipment set Tank". A second piece only as good as what you wear is not.
- Fixed: things you use and keep were advised for sale, such as a Cozy Sleeping Bag. An item with a Use: of its own that doesn't stack and has no charges to use up (a sleeping bag, a toy, a hearthstone, a trinket with a use) is kept: "Keep: you use it". So is anything Unique that isn't grey ("unique, and yours"), however much the auction house would pay. Potions, food and anything else that stacks follow the usual advice.
- The other faction on the target frame again: its badge and outline went missing on a target frame showing incoming heals (the bar clipped them). They're on a layer of their own now, and the health bar is lit in the faction's colour as well. **The other faction: colours** (`/cchud factionstyle`): the faction's own colours (Alliance blue, Horde red) or Ally or enemy (an enemy's red, whichever faction).
- No more Lua error with raid marks the game keeps secret ("attempt to compare field 'raidIndex'"): the mark was drawn, but noting it as shown compared it with the last one.
- Big moments are brief and read at a glance: the abilities in one line, in the order they landed, with how many hits ("Judgement + Seal of Command + Holy Strike  ×3"), and under them the damage, big. It's the total where the game lets an addon add it up; where it keeps the amounts to itself (an addon may show them but not add them), each hit side by side, "246 · 213 · 104". On screen about as long as a crit's flash, a little longer for a longer combo (two seconds at most), then gone.
- Seals call out as they run out every time. In a fight the game often keeps a buff's time to itself, and then a Seal only popped once it was gone. Now it runs out as long after you put it on as it lasted when its time could be read, and a recast starts the clock over. A Seal that went on before your spellbook was read at login was also left out for the whole session; it's asked about again now.
- Fixed: using an item from the bag window (a scroll, a potion, food) made the game block ControllerCam: HUD ("blocked from an action only available to the Blizzard UI"). A use now always goes through the game's own secure item button, from your own press. X is now Use (at a vendor it sells, at the bank it moves the item) and A picks up and puts down. On a bag, X shows only that bag and A puts what you hold into it. With the mouse, right click uses and left click or a drag picks up. In a fight the window takes no buttons, as before, so nothing can be used from it until the fight ends.
- Fixed: at the mailbox (and a vendor, the bank, a trade or the auction house) LT and RT no longer leave the controller stuck in the bag window. They move between the windows the way the Gamepad UI does: the bag window has the controller while the game's focus is on your bags, and lets it go while the focus is on the other window, whose own buttons (A Select, X Open All, B Back) then work as the game made them. Where the game has no window to move to, the press hands the controller over itself. The bag window's hint names where the triggers go ("LT or RT: to the mail", "to the vendor", "to the bank"...), and says "back to bags" while the other window has it.
- The bag window puts what you use first (setting: **Bag window: order**, `/cchud bagorder`, buttons **Smart** (the default), **By type** and **As in your bags**). Smart puts consumables and anything with a Use: (potions, food, scrolls, the hearthstone, an on-use trinket) first. The rest follows by kind and then name, so nothing moves about while your bags stay as they are, and junk comes last. By type sorts your items into sections, each under its own heading with a count: Consumables, Equipment, Trade goods, Quest, Reagents, Other, Junk, and your empty slots. The D-pad runs from one section into the next. As in your bags keeps the game's own order. In every order, junk is greyed and faded, with its coin. Junk is what you marked as junk, grey items, and what the HUD's advice sends to a vendor once the auction house prices are known, never a quest item or anything you chose to keep. Quest items have the game's gold quest edge, or its "!" while they start a quest you haven't taken. The Junk filter goes by the same rule.
- The bag window shows your empty slots as empty slots at the end of the grid, instead of one slot with a count, so you can see how much room is left and put something down in any of them. The count of free slots stays beside your bags.
- Each bag in the bag window's bag row shows its slots in the corner, as a stack shows its count: "12/16" (used of how many) where that fits in the icon, else "16". The backpack too.
- Destroy from the bag window: Y on an item, then **Destroy**. It can't be undone, so the item goes onto the cursor and a panel beside the window asks, naming it and its stack ("Destroy Linen Cloth x20?"). Hold A until the bar fills, then let go, and the game destroys it. Let go early, or press B, close the window or get into a fight, and nothing is destroyed and the item goes back. With the mouse, the panel's **Hold to destroy** button works the same way. It isn't offered for bound gear in one of your equipment sets, or for a quest item a quest of yours still needs.
- Your bags work in a fight. Open the bag window mid-fight (or keep it open as a fight starts) and the D-pad, X, A and B keep working: X drinks a potion, eats, reads a scroll, or equips a weapon, shield or off hand, all at once with no question. Smart order puts your potions first and the window opens on the first slot, so it's your bag key and then X. Armour can't be changed in a fight, so X on it says "Armour can't be changed in a fight." instead of trying. B closes your bags and gives the controller back at once. In a fight the grid stays as it was, each slot showing what is in it now, and it is put in order again once the fight ends. Everything goes through the game's own secure buttons, so nothing is ever blocked.
- Cinematic flights: the camera now circles slowly round you and your mount for as long as the film plays, always the same way round, a whole turn in about a minute and a half, easing in as the film comes in. About half a minute before you land it comes round behind you again, the shorter way, and is settled there as the weather is said, so you see yourself come in. The flight game, a window or your own hand on the controller stops it at once (the camera is yours), and it is always stopped when the film ends, however it ends. While the film plays, ControllerCam also rests the game's camera auto-follow, which pulled the camera back behind you as you flew, and puts it back after (even if the game closed mid-flight). A switch of its own, **Cinematic flights: the camera circles you** (`/cchud flightorbit`, on by default), leaves it a still shot. Where the game refuses to turn the camera, the film carries on as a still shot.
- The minimap's own marks no longer stay on screen through a cinematic flight or the campfire scene: your arrow, the quest arrows and the dots the game draws on it don't fade with the rest of the screen, so the whole minimap now hides once the screen has faded and comes back, its terrain drawn again, as the screen does. Only what was showing comes back (a minimap you had hidden stays hidden), and it is never hidden in a fight.

ControllerCam: HUD
- Nameplates your way. **Nameplate size**: Small, Normal or Large bars (`/cchud platesize`). **Names on nameplates**: in full, short, or none, with the level and class icon kept (the target frame has the full name; a friendly NPC with no bar always keeps its name; `/cchud platename`). **Nameplate look**: the switches in one press. The target of your target wears the other faction's badge too.
- Sell unless there is a reason not to. An item's advice is now to sell it, at the auction house where that pays and to a vendor otherwise, unless it is an upgrade for you or another character of yours, a profession of one of yours wants it, or a quest you can still do wants it. Each of those says which. Gear about as good as what you wear is no reason to keep it any more ("it could be an upgrade for you" is gone). What you wear says "you wear it", and your one tool of its kind, your best potion of its kind and what you chose to keep are kept as before.
- Upgrades for your other characters: "Keep  Upgrade for Sol". The HUD now notes what each of your characters wears, their level and their side whenever you play them, and weighs an item for each of them by their class: only one who can put it on now (their level, their class's weapons, the armor they wear) and is clearly better off for it. Only your characters on this realm and of your side, as the mail goes no further, and never for an item bound to you. A character counts once you have logged in with them on this version. These items are not sold by your junk rules either.
- An upgrade that binds when equipped and isn't bound yet also says what it fetches at the auction house: "Equip  Upgrade: Holy  ·  worth 12g 40s at auction", on the tooltip and the Market, and the same for an upgrade for another character of yours. Where that is clearly more than wearing it is worth, it says so: "worth 15g at auction: more than it is worth to wear". That is when the auction house pays at least 25 silver for each of your levels (10g 50s at 42) and ten times what a vendor pays. Gear that binds when picked up, or is already bound to you, says nothing of the sort.
- Disenchant: green, blue and purple gear that an enchanter of yours would rather disenchant than sell says "Disenchant  Enchanting 120/150, Sol" and how much more it brings. Under it are the materials it gives (dust, essence or shard, how many on average and how often) and what each fetches at the auction house. It counts when the materials bring clearly more than selling the item, or when one of them still gives that enchanter a skill-up. Only for an enchanter with the skill for it, and for an item bound to you only when you are the enchanter. Gear only an enchanter wants is kept from the sell window and your junk rules.
- Quests you haven't picked up yet: an item a quest you can still do wants says which, with its level and place: "Quest: [35] Stranglethorn Fever 4/10 in Stranglethorn Vale". The game tells an addon only of the quests in your log, so the HUD now carries a short list of quests that ask for items you can trade or sell (about a hundred, from Gorilla Fangs, Shrunken Heads and the Green Hills of Stranglethorn pages to Wastewander Water Pouches, Un'Goro Soil and the Blasted Lands' brew reagents). Only quests for your side, not grey for you and not done yet count; once you outlevel one or hand it in, the item is sold like any other. Quests in your log say the same way: "[30] The Weaver's Cloth 3/10 in Duskwood". The quest and zone names are in English for now. The switch **Keep what your quests need** covers these too.
- One bag window, made for the controller (switch: **One bag window with quick filters**, `/cchud bagwindow`, on by default). Your bags open in a window of the HUD's own instead of the game's Combined Backpack. Everything you carry sits in one grid, in the order of your bags. The empty slots are gathered at the end as one slot that says how many there are (a quiver or herb bag gets its own). Your bags sit in a row of their own under the grid, not among your items. LB and RB go round the quick filters along the top: All, Equipment, Consumables, Trade goods, Quest, Junk, New, and the HUD's advice, Sell and Keep. The D-pad moves through the grid and down to the bags. A uses the item, the same as the game's right click (at a vendor it sells, at the bank it moves the item, at the mailbox it attaches it). X sells at a vendor, and elsewhere picks the item up and puts it down again. Y has more: split a stack, mark it, its details, sort, the bank. B closes. On a bag, A shows only that bag and X puts what you hold into it. While you stand at the bank, the back button shows the bank in the same window. With a vendor, the bank or the mailbox open too, LT or RT passes the controller to that window and back. In a fight the window stays open, as the game's bags do, but the controller is yours until the fight ends. Every slot is the game's own item button, so the mouse works as before. Off: the game's own bags.
- A healer's party and raid (setting: **Healer's party and raid**, `/cchud healerframes`, buttons **Off**, **In a healing spec** (the default) and **Always**). In a healing spec (the talent tree with the most points is Holy for a paladin, Discipline or Holy for a priest, Restoration for a shaman or druid) your party sits right beside your character, between it and the cooldown callouts, one slim row a member. At each row's left end is the button that targets them, the Gamepad UI's own: LB and the D-pad direction the game gives that member. Each row shows only what a healer needs: their name and health, with how much they are missing ("-700") and the empty part of the bar in red, amber under 70% and red under 35%. Beside it is a debuff you can cure, the game's own icon edged in its kind's colour, and only the kinds your class cures (a paladin: poison and disease with Purify, magic too once Cleanse is learnt). Whoever is out of range is dimmed, and whoever is too far to target at all is dimmed further and says "Too far". In a raid the game's raid frames keep their places and their LB + D-pad navigation; our look over each is now a slimmer tile a little inside it, with the same three things and nothing else (no power bar, no percent). Our party frames only move out of combat: a spec changed in a fight takes its look when the fight ends. Line of sight can't be read by an addon, so it isn't shown.
- Cinematic flights (switch: **Cinematic flights**, `/cchud flightscene`, on by default). On a flight master's flight the screen turns to film a few seconds after take-off, as at a camp fire: black bars close in, the rest of the screen fades back, and the camera draws out wide and swings slowly round you and your mount, out to one side, behind you and out to the other. Where you are going comes up in the bottom bar ("Booty Bay", and under it "Stranglethorn  ·  Evening"), and each new zone you fly into after it. The flight bar rises into the top bar and stays readable there, with the flight game's line under it. Starting the flight game steps out of the film at once and its lane plays over the screen as ever; put it away at high altitude and the film comes back after a moment. Opening a window (the map, your bags) or pressing a button or the camera stick steps out too, and the film comes back once the controller has been left alone for a few seconds. It bows out as you come in to land, just before the weather where you land is said, the camera swinging back behind you and in to the distance it had, so you see yourself come in; on a route whose time isn't known yet, it ends at the landing. A fight, the landing or a reload in the air end it at once and put the screen, the flight bar, the camera and its shoulder offset back as they were. Flights too short for it (under about 45 seconds) are left alone, and with Reduce animations on the camera stays still and the bars fade in where they stand.
- A quest compass (switch: **Quest compass**, `/cchud compass`, on by default). A thin strip along the top of the screen shows the way you face, with N, E, S and W and the points between, and your quests where they lie. Each has a mark: the HUD's gold mark for a quest still to do, the game's question mark for one to hand in, and your own map pin. Under each mark is how far it is ("140 yd"). Turn and the marks slide across. They come in as you turn towards them and fade out at the strip's ends. Nearer ones are larger and clearer, and the quest the game points you to has a soft glow and its distance in gold. A focused quest in another zone shows the way the game leads you there. It shows the quests you track that have a place in this zone, the six nearest (all of yours here when you track none of them). Where two distances would overlap, only the nearer one is shown, and a letter steps back under a mark. In a fight the strip steps back too. It stays clear of your buffs and of the game's widgets at the top middle (a battleground's score). It steps away on a flight, while Edit Mode is open, and where the game doesn't say where you are or which way you face (inside a dungeon). **Quest compass: place** (`/cchud compassplace`, buttons **Top** and **Bottom**) puts it at the bottom in the experience bar's place, and the bar goes to the top. The bar only moves out of combat. Edit Mode gets it back while open, and it goes back where the game had it when the compass is off or at the top, or the HUD stops. The compass follows your character's facing, as an addon can't read the camera's; with the camera's "Turn character with camera" that is where you look. `/cchud compassdemo` puts a few marks around you for twenty seconds.

- Shapeshift forms (druid forms, ghost wolf) no longer push the camera further to the side: the game scales the shoulder offset with your model, so in a form it is scaled back. `/ccam formoffset <percent>` tunes it for the form you are in.
- Two more situations: **Indoors** and **Fishing** (a fishing pole in hand, or the fishing cast). Both are empty until you pick a profile for them, for example `/ccam set indoors shooter` for a closer over-the-shoulder view inside buildings.

ControllerCam: HUD (new, optional second addon in the same download)
- It asks before it does anything: the first time it loads beside ControllerCam it rests and asks **Camera + HUD** or **Camera only**. Until you answer, and after "Camera only", the game's interface is exactly as it was. `/cchud on`, `/cchud off` or the Enabled switch in its options answer the question just the same, and `/cchud welcome` asks again. Installed on its own, or with settings from before, it does not ask. A second question follows a yes, and comes once to anyone using the HUD on one of the game's built-in layouts: shall the screen be set up for the controller? It adds a layout named CCHUD to the game's Edit Mode, with the cooldown rows under the target and clear of the gamepad buttons, for you to choose there (the game does not let addons choose a layout, so you are told where to pick it); your own layouts are kept, and someone who arranged a layout of their own is not asked. `/cchud setup` offers it again. Picking a preset adds it too if you are still on a built-in layout, and never overwrites one you have arranged since. `/cchud reset all` puts everything back to how it started: every setting, the frames, and that layout as it was made; your vendor marks and price history are kept.
- Part of the ControllerCam family: its options sit under ControllerCam's (Options > AddOns > ControllerCam > HUD, with its pages under it), the addon list groups it with ControllerCam on clients that group addons, and its main page starts with a Modules list to switch each part on or off. Without the camera addon it keeps its own place in the options.
- A controller interface that takes over while the Gamepad UI is in use and gives everything back in the mouse and keyboard UI, or when it is turned off or removed.
- Slim player, pet, target, target-of-target and focus frames with class colours, a damage trail, hit flashes, a low-health pulse and a combat glow. They can be dragged in the game's Edit Mode.
- Party and raid frames, nameplates and the cast bar get the same look, drawn over the game's own frames, which keep doing the targeting and keep their options and positions.
- If our party and raid look cannot find the game's member frames, the game's own frames stay in sight instead of disappearing.
- The game's own player, target and focus frames are made invisible but stay where the game keeps them, so the controller's unit menu (leave party, inspect, trade...) keeps working.
- Party frames no longer break when the game keeps a party member's range or status secret ("attempt to perform boolean test on a secret boolean"): range fading is then done by the game itself, unseen by us.
- NPCs whose job is in their name ("Auctioneer Tricket", "Innkeeper Norman") get their icon too.
- Party frames of our own when the game keeps its party frames hidden in the Gamepad UI: secure (click to target, the unit menu), stacked on the left under the focus frame.
- Hit effects also fire from a health drop on your target and on nameplates, for clients that send addons no hit events.
- Nameplates show only your own debuffs (a client that ignores the "yours" filter no longer shows everyone's); the quest marker is the icon alone; a name can no longer go missing on your target's plate.
- Enemy casts on nameplates start with a flash and a swell, then pulse ever faster until they land (calm for casts that cannot be interrupted).
- Threat as a shield icon and a colour, on nameplates and the target frame alike: red and strong when it is on you (shield with "!"), red and yellow in turn when you are about to pull it (arrow up), yellow and blue in turn when you are losing it (arrow down), blue when it is not on you. The colour glows behind the plate's name and around the target bar. Your share as a number beside the icon is optional (`/cchud threatpercent`). The level keeps its difficulty colour. Without threat numbers, the unit's own target tells it.
- Rank in the classic style: gold wings around an elite's name, silver for a rare, silver wings on a gold glow for a rare elite, and a skull with gold wings for a boss.
- Compact nameplates and a compact target frame (about 60% of the width).
- Nameplates show a power bar only for units that have a resource (no empty track under monsters that never build rage or energy).
- The screen rests out of combat, all or nothing: the gamepad bars, the minimap, the quest list, the damage meter and the experience bar fade together (the game has no option for it) and come back together for a fight, a pulled trigger (and a few seconds after: a pull of the trigger is how you look at them), an enemy target, the controller's cursor or the mouse over one of them. One switch (**Fade out of combat**) and one level (**The screen at rest**); each part can be left out under Advanced. What you had chosen for the gamepad bars' own fade carries over. `/cchud compact` switches the bars between compact and full (the game's own Edit Mode setting), also from the options.
- The stance and pet command buttons move up to where the green and red targeting icons sat, and those icons are hidden: the wheel a button opens (stances, forms, pet commands) is no longer cut off by the bottom of the screen. The buttons stay the game's own; they are moved out of combat only, and put back in Edit Mode or with **Stance and pet buttons above the bars** off. `/cchud padinfo` lists where the bars' parts sit, for troubleshooting.
- Debug reports, off unless you switch them on (`/cchud debug on`): the troubleshooting reports then gather themselves when you log in and again at the moment you reload or log out, with short notes of what the HUD did in between, all in the saved settings. Nothing leaves your computer.
- Your bars have an in-combat level (70% by default) and come back in full at a call to action: health below 50% or mana below 30%. The focus frame steps back out of combat too.
- Minimal keeps your focus and pet frames and hides them out of combat; Action fades them.
- The game's pop-up buff tooltip is switched off once (it covered the target frame); switch it back on in the options.
- Effects are read the way this client allows: the unit's whole list, with the game itself picking which are yours and which are crowd control (reading them one by one is refused when their details are secret, which hid your debuffs). Clients without those calls still read them one by one, and an effect from a spell you cast lately counts as yours there. `/cchud aurareport` lists what the game says, for troubleshooting.
- The quest marker sits on the health bar's line.
- No more hooks on the game's own frames (gamepad bars, player, target and focus frames, nameplates): our alpha is kept by a timer of our own instead, so the game's code is never tainted ("Interface action failed because of an AddOn").
- Nameplate names stay white and readable at their usual size: threat shows in a strong coloured glow behind the name, which flares when you take aggro and fades when you lose it; with aggro the name is bolder.
- Crowd control on a nameplate sits at the bar's right end, after the threat shield and the quest marker (each moves up when the one before it is not showing), instead of on the left over the level and name.
- Effect countdowns turn red under five seconds, on nameplates and the target frame (the game's own icons and ours alike).
- Effect times no longer overlap: under the target the time sits below each icon with room between them, the game's countdown numbers are drawn smaller, and nameplate icons are spaced a little wider.
- Effects are drawn by the game itself where this client offers it (its aura display element): your debuffs (judgements, Consecration...), the enemy's buffs and crowd control on nameplates and the target frame, with their time and stacks, even when their details are secret to addons. Clients without it keep our own rows.
- "Name on your target's plate" now means the same for every target (before, an enemy attacking you kept its name, so names came and went in a fight). It is switched back on once by this update. A plate whose rank the game keeps secret in a fight still shows its name.
- Stylized numbers for what you take: damage pops in the game's damage font, coloured by its school, and falls away beside your character; heals rise in green; a burst spreads out so each number stays readable. Crits, dodges, parries and misses are the big moments: they slam in huge, stick where they land, then rise and fade, like your own crits. They stand in for the game's scrolling combat text for you and follow its option (off there: off here).
- The game's own frames in the HUD's look while the Gamepad UI is in use (Options > Look, each part on its own switch): the gamepad buttons get a slim ring on a dark backing instead of the heavy art, with a soft pool behind each cluster; tooltips become dark glass edged in a player's class colour or an item's quality; buffs are cropped and edged; the quest tracker's gold headers become glass; the minimap gets a slim ring and halo instead of the gold rim; the experience bar is drawn smaller in its own look; chat tabs and the chat box follow. Nothing of the game's is hooked or replaced: the frames keep working, and switching off, going back to the mouse UI or removing the addon puts every one back as it was.
- `/chud` works as a short name for `/cchud`.
- Controller vibration: a thud when you are hit (heavier for a big hit or a crit), a tick in the right trigger for your crits, a double knock when your target's cast is interrupted, a heartbeat while you are low in a fight, a pulse when your target turns on you, alerts for a ready check, a dungeon invitation or a whisper, a swell at level up and a long rumble at death. Each on its own switch, with a strength slider; the game's own vibration setting comes first (off there: nothing). Putting on a seal charges up: a quick rising rumble that snaps in the right trigger. Optional (off by default): spells with a cast time build up with the cast bar and snap when they land. `/cchud rumbletest` plays every pattern once.
- Signature moments: each class's key abilities have a feel of their own on the controller (a Judgement splashes, a Holy Strike snaps, a Windfury Attack knocks three times; Mortal Strike, Eviscerate, Pyroblast, Aimed Shot, Stormstrike and more), stronger on a crit. Rarely, a really special hit is pinned big and golden in the middle of the screen in the game's damage font, with the ability's name: back-to-back crits of your key abilities, a Windfury crit, or a crit far above the fight's usual; at most twice a fight. `/cchud momenttest` shows one.
- A controller with a light bar (DualSense) can show your health in it: green to red, pulsing when you are low in a fight; it goes back to the game's colour when switched off.
- Casts look like what they are, on your cast bar and the enemies': mining throws chips of stone, herbs shed leaves, skinning, opening, crafting, disenchanting, hearthstones and mounts each have their colour and their little effects, and a spell takes the colour of its school (learned from the combat log) and lands in that school's style.
- Cast bar animations, painted in the game's own style (a dark line, a few flat tones, a glint): picks peck at the end of the bar while you mine (chips fly at every strike) and a hammer while you craft, leaves sprout along it for herbs, green lightning runs along it stroke after stroke for nature and pale blue lightning for lightning spells, roots creep along it for the spells that root a foe, a vine winds along it and flowers open one after another for a heal (gold ones for a holy heal), runes hover over it and take shape one by one for arcane, flames burn along what is filled and grow as the cast builds, ice freezes on crystal by crystal and shatters when the cast lands, hooded hollow-eyed shades drift through a dark haze for shadow, rays rise for holy, and a physical cast keeps the plain bar with only a glint of steel. Every school has its show, and each is kept to a few pieces. Your own bar has the full show, nameplates a lighter one. `/cchud casttest` plays them all.
- Spell schools are known from the start for the spells players cast most (by name, so every rank and language), and learned for the rest from the combat log or from the hit or heal that follows your cast, on clients whose combat log addons cannot read. A spell shows its school's effect while it is cast as well as when it lands.
- Healing you can see: the heal on its way to a unit takes the colour of the heal being cast at it (holy gold, nature green; yours or a group member's), and a heal over time grows over the bar of your target, focus or you while it lasts: roots and branches for nature (Rejuvenation, Regrowth, Lifebloom...), golden threads for holy (Renew). They sway gently and wither away when it ends.
- Heals light up the bar they land on in their school's colour (holy, nature...), with its effect rising off it: your target, focus, nameplates and your own bars.
- Your player frame and vertical bars give a small shake when you are hit (switch: Your bars shake when you are hit).
- The threat shield sits on the health bar's line, just past its end (on the target frame and nameplates, where the quest marker steps aside for it), so the name above has the bar's full width; over the compact target bar it has more room than the bar. The target of target moves out past both, so nothing overlaps.
- A nameplate the game reuses for another unit no longer keeps the last unit's "Interrupted".
- Casts the game names by their inner workings show a plain name: "Opening - No Text" reads "Opening", and opening or gathering says what you work on when the game can name it ("Opening: Sweet Nectar Barrel").
- Enemy cast bars show the spell's icon (greyed when it cannot be interrupted); an interrupted cast shatters in red and says so, a cast that lands bursts, and the bar lingers a moment so you see which.
- Effects drawn by the game (debuffs, buffs, crowd control on nameplates and the target) now show their icons; only their timers showed before.
- Picking a camera preset for everywhere in ControllerCam also picks the matching HUD preset (Action, Classic, Shooter, Raid... each has its match; the camera keeps its own distance). Switch: Follow ControllerCam's presets.
- Health on the bars: full-size target and nameplates show the health left and the percent ("12.3k  85%"), compact ones the percent alone; the target's number sits on its bar, clear of the name.
- Small plates for minor units now include grey enemies that give no experience, and show the name and a slim bar only (no level, rank flourish or health text). Compact bars are switched back to full size once.
- `/cchud errors` lists the last errors the HUD met, also after a reload, to report them.
- Quick switches after the fact: the HUD's main page has a Quick switches section (tooltips at the cursor, compact bars everywhere or one by one), and a key binding flips tooltips at the cursor on and off; also `/cchud compactall` and `/cchud targetcompact`; presets never change it.
- Class icons for players, friend or enemy, on the target frame and now on nameplates too, on by default; presets no longer switch them off, and they come back on once for anyone a preset had switched them off for.
- Presets always give the same result, whatever was chosen before: each one starts from the defaults of everything any preset sets. Most keep the full name data on plates (player titles, guilds and what NPCs do, such as flight masters and trainers); only Soulslike, Shooter and ARPG go without guilds and jobs.
- Presets: four more whole-screen layouts after other kinds of games (Adventure, Crossbar, ARPG, Compass); every preset keeps what you need to play (nameplates with names, levels, threat, debuffs, crowd control and loud casts, your vitals, your target, the cast bar, party frames); Minimal shows friendly NPCs only when you point at them, aim at them or target them; and a preset brings the camera to the distance its layout was made for, once (switch that off in General).
- Players' full names everywhere: the target, target of target, focus, party frames and nameplates all read "Faith Ashborne", last name included (the game hands it over as "Faith-Ashborne", sometimes already joined; the target frame used to drop the last name). A name that already carries its last name (a player's title name does) never gets it twice. Switch: Last names. `/cchud names` shows what the game gives for your target's name.
- The numbers for what you take appear at the vertical bars when those are on, falling away outward.
- The experience bar keeps its own look, drawn smaller (size in Look).
- The minimap's ring is drawn large and sharp; the chat box's glass shows only while you type.
- Bags: sort with one action (a key binding under AddOns, or `/cchud sort`), the bank too while it is open; it is the game's own sorting, so its options count. An item's tooltip says how many you have in your bags and bank and how many your other characters hold, with the total. `/cchud gold` lists every character's gold.
- Junk is judged more carefully: profession tools and fishing poles (a mining pick is a white weapon to the game), gear with a use of its own, and white weapons or armor that could be an upgrade for you (an empty slot, or a higher item level than what you wear) are never junk, unless you mark them as junk yourself. Selling when a vendor opens only sells grey items and your own junk marks by itself; anything else is listed and asked about first. `/cchud sold` lists what was sold this session, for the vendor's Buyback tab.
- Item tooltips show what an item sells for at a vendor, each and for a full stack, and the lowest auction buyout you have seen for it, with how long ago (noted from whatever the auction house shows you, old or new auction house alike).
- Tooltips say more in fewer words, in colour: item counts read "Bag x26  Bank x40  (x80)" (the total across your characters in gold), prices are in the coins' colours, and each stat value has a colour of its own (attack power, ranged attack power, crit, dodge, health, mana, spell crit).
- What stats are worth to you: each stat line in an item's tooltip gets its value for your character in brackets, "+10 Agility (+10 AP, +20 RAP, +0.19% crit, +0.38% dodge)": Strength and Agility into attack power and ranged attack power by class, Agility into crit and dodge, Stamina into health, Intellect into mana and spell crit. Crit and dodge per point come from the game for your level where it says them. Works in every language.
- Market, a tab of the auction house (L3 opens it from the controller): what in your bags is worth selling there, first and marked green, each with the price we would ask and why (how much more than a vendor it brings, and how the price was chosen); then the rest. Up and down choose, A posts, X switches patient and quick sale, Y how long, B goes back to the house; with a mouse, point at a row and click it. Beside the house a pane that only shows follows your choice: the item's price chart for the last 30 days, its usual price, how many are up, what a vendor pays and the reasoning in full. Posting is always your press.
- The auction house is read whole on your first visit in a while (the game allows it every 15 minutes; `/cchud scan` asks for it), a slice at a time so nothing stutters. Each item's tooltip then says its best use, with an icon: the auction house (and how much more that brings after the house's cut than a vendor would pay), a vendor, or keep it (your kept marks, quest items, possible upgrades).
- Friendly NPCs show what they are by kind: banker, guild banker, auctioneer, flight master, innkeeper, stable master, battlemaster, trainer, and vendors of food, reagents, poisons, ammunition or repairs each have their own icon (the game's map-tracking icons), read from the line under the NPC's name; others keep the game's interaction icon.
- Only the effects that matter (on by default), on the target frame and nameplates:
  - An enemy's buffs show only when the game marks them important (boss mechanics among them), when it is enraged, or when you can dispel or steal them. Buffs that are always there are left out.
  - On an enemy you see your own debuffs plus the ones the game marks important.
  - A friendly target now shows its debuffs on the right under its bar: the ones you can dispel, edged in their kind's colour, then boss mechanics, then crowd control.
  - Turn it off to see every buff again.
- Nameplates in dungeons: no more errors where the game keeps units' details secret, and no more plates left showing someone who has gone (a reused plate is cleared first, and plates are checked again every second). The aura icons above a plate stay in one short line, clear of its name.
- The game's own friendly nameplates (off by default): leave your party, other players and friendly NPCs to the game's plates; ours stay on enemies.
- R3 at the bank sorts the bank (the tab you are on, your character's or your account's, with the game's own Clean Up Bank) unless the cursor is in your bags; press it again within a few seconds to sort your bags too. Every sort says what it sorted.
- Deposit puts away what the bank already holds: every item in your bags that the bank has some of goes in, along with the items you marked for the bank (never grey items, tools, or items you marked as junk or kept). It's a key binding under AddOns, `/cchud deposit`, or automatic when the bank opens, and you can turn the matching off.
- Item details: point at an item and use the new "Item details" key binding (or `/cchud item`, or `/cchud item <link or id>`) for one window with everything known about it: level, stack size, item ID, vendor and auction prices, how many you and your other characters hold, and your mark on it. B or Escape closes it.
- The stick buttons work the bag windows: while your bags or the bank are open, R3 sorts them; while the bank, a vendor, a trade or the mailbox is open, L3 moves the item you point at (into or out of the bank, sold, into the trade, onto the letter). They are put back the moment those windows close, and never changed in a fight. Quick move works in a trade and at the mailbox too.
- Your whole setup is one line of text you can keep or share (`/cchud export`, `/cchud import`, or the buttons in the options): every HUD setting, which frames are on, where they sit, your vendor rules and marks, ControllerCam's camera setup (which profile each situation uses, your own profiles, the built-in ones you edited) and the game's own Edit Mode layout. Import only takes known settings with values of the right kind and range, refuses a line that was cut short or changed, adds the layout to the game's own for you to choose in Edit Mode, and says what it took and what it left as it was.
- Repairs everything as soon as a vendor who repairs opens (from the guild bank first if you switch that on and your guild allows it).
- Chat shows who is talking as `[icon level] Name: text`: a player's class icon and level in brackets in front of their name, when the game lets us know them (your group, guild and friends, players you have seen around or looked up with /who). The name stays the link it was. Gold sellers and adverts with a web address are hidden, and so is the same message repeated by one player within two minutes; never from your group, guild or friends. `/cchud spam` lists what was hidden.
- Friendly NPCs that offer something wear the game's own icon for it above their name: a bag over vendors, an anvil over smiths who repair, a book over trainers. They show over every such NPC (the game shows one only for what you are pointing at), and stay up even while a townsperson's plate waits to be pointed at. Switch: What NPCs do, as icons.
- Nameplates: an option (off by default) to show friendly NPCs' plates only while you point at them, like their tooltip.
- The demo shows everything without a target: your debuffs, the enemy's buffs and crowd control, aggro, hit effects of every school and the numbers on you. `/cchud hittest` plays on your own frame when nothing is targeted.
- Nameplates show effects like the game's own: the debuffs it marks for nameplates (yours only by default, or everyone's), the enemy's buffs, and crowd control as a large icon with its time beside the bar. The target frame shows an enemy's buffs on the right.
- Targeting yourself brings your bars back in full; low health or mana shows them even out of combat. Minimal keeps names and rests your bars, focus, pet and gamepad bars as a faint hint out of combat instead of hiding them.
- Tooltips follow your cursor (the Gamepad UI's cursor too) instead of sitting in the corner of the screen; in a fight they stay in the corner.
- Nameplates show player titles, guilds and NPC jobs, health and power bars, and casts with who they are aimed at. Friends show only their name until they are in a fight. Every part can be switched off.
- Hit effects: every hit you or your pet land makes the bar it lands on flash in the colour of its damage school and shake with that school's character (fire flickers, frost glints, shadow sways, arcane pulses twice). Crits hit harder. Works on the target, focus and nameplates.
- Text is sized for a TV by default (150%, `/cchud text` from 80 to 250); the bars move down as the text grows, so a big name never runs into its bar.
- In a fight, health bars turn from green through yellow to red as health drops.
- Nameplates: the game's own plate stays hidden under ours; optional health number (12.3k) and percent; your debuffs above the name; an aggro glow (red while it attacks you, orange when you are about to pull it); low health breathes; crowd control shimmers; plates fade in.
- Hit effects also work when the game keeps damage numbers secret. `/cchud hittest` shows every school once.
- Every damage school has its own particles: holy rays, rising fire, lingering frost crystals, drifting shadow smoke, twinkling arcane runes, nature motes, physical sparks.
- Your cast bar winds up (the glow builds and pulses before it lands) and bursts when the cast lands.
- Fishing: your cast bar becomes the line, thin and the colour of water, with a float bobbing at its end and rings spreading around it, and the fish likely to bite there (read from Improved Forever's fishing data when that addon is loaded; otherwise the place you are fishing).
- The vertical bars follow the camera, to stay beside your character (on by default), and gently: they move by how much your character grows on screen rather than by their whole distance from the middle, come in only a quarter of the way however far the camera pulls back, hold their place when you come closer than three yards or into first person, glide instead of jumping, and never leave the screen.

## 1.1.0

- Localization is complete: the profile descriptions, tooltips, messages and the `/ccam help` text are translated in all ten game languages (they were English before).
- Descriptions where you pick a camera: hover any profile button in the options to see what it feels like and every setting it changes, and `/ccam describe <profile>` prints the same. The options show the description of the profile in use.
- Advanced editing: tick "Advanced: edit built-in profiles" on the Profiles page (or `/ccam advanced on`) to change any setting of a built-in profile. Your changes are kept, an edited profile is marked "(edited)", and "Reset to original" (`/ccam reset <profile>`) brings the original back. Without Advanced the built-in profiles stay locked, and copies of them can always be edited.

## 1.0.0

- First release.
- An Action camera out of the box: close third-person view over your shoulder, head tracking, the camera follows where you run.
- Built-in profiles: Action, Hybrid, Classic, Shooter, Tactical, Cinematic, Target focus, Raid view, Mounted cruise, Flying and Town stroll. `/ccam use <name>` switches the whole game to one of them.
- A profile for each situation: vehicle, flying, swimming, mounted, combat, raid, dungeon, city or inn, and everywhere else. A fresh install uses a recommended mix (`/ccam starter`).
- Shoulder offset that follows the camera zoom (Action: +2 at normal distances, smaller when zoomed in so the character stays on screen, fading to 0 when zoomed out), mirror option for the other shoulder. `/ccam info` shows what the camera is doing.
- Custom profiles: save your current camera, copy and edit built-in ones, share with versioned, validated import/export strings.
- The camera's terrain following is off in the built-in profiles (except Classic); a profile that does not mention it leaves your own setting alone.
- Options panel under Options > AddOns > ControllerCam, with a Profiles page that edits every setting.
- Settings that survive the UI reload the Gamepad UI switch causes (your original camera values and the motion-sickness settings are saved), and CVars the game refuses in combat are applied when combat ends.
- Localization: German, Spanish (Spain and Mexico), French, Italian, Korean, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Simplified Chinese and Traditional Chinese for the interface. Long profile descriptions and the help text are English for now.
